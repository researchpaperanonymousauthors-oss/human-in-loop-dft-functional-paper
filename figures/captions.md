# Paper B3 figure captions (draft v1; numbers checked by check_numbers.py)

**Figure 1. The tool.** (a) The loop a person follows in the page. The cheap GFN2-xTB map is shown first. The person clicks a point or takes one of up to four suggestions. The page then reveals one Skala-1.1/def2-SVP energy and updates the map correction, the well energies, the pass and the suggestions. The page is in replay mode: the 665 energies were computed in advance and a click looks one up. Every click is written to a session log. (b) The page after 10 clicks of a simulated clicker. 1, the estimated map with the revealed points (white circles) and the suggestions (orange squares); 2, the suggestions; 3, the barrier estimate between two named wells; 4, the session log; 5, the statement that the page is in replay mode.

**Figure 2. The system and the point plan.** (a) A frozen nitrite frame (N-O 1.24 angstrom, O-N-O 115 degrees) and one proton. The proton position is given by the angle theta from the mirror axis and the distance rho from the centroid of the three frame atoms. Dashed circles: the 0.8 angstrom wall. The three wells are marked; only theta from 0 to 180 degrees is computed, the faint markers are mirror images. (b) The planned points on the xTB map: 214 coarse points (10 degrees, 0.10 angstrom) and 451 fine points (5 degrees, 0.05 angstrom, below 60 kcal/mol on the xTB map). White: wall. This is a proton-position map, not a relaxed reaction path.

**Figure 3. Skala-1.1/def2-SVP against the cheap map.** (a) GFN2-xTB map. (b) Skala map from all 665 points; the dashed line is the lowest path between the cis and trans wells, and the star is its highest point (43.0 kcal/mol above the cis well). White: wall or no Skala point. (c) Skala minus xTB after removing the mean offset of 2.9 kcal/mol over the 306 points below 40 kcal/mol; the black line marks 40 kcal/mol on the Skala map. (d) Well energies above the cis well. Skala puts the trans well (4.4) below the nitro well (8.5); xTB (9.5 and 5.2) and PBE (5.4 and 3.7) put them in the opposite order. No higher-level reference was computed, so we do not say which order is correct. (e) The cis to trans pass: 34.4 kcal/mol with the xTB map alone, 41.7 with the 214 coarse Skala points and 43.0 with all 665 points.

**Figure 4. How the estimate improves.** Estimated Skala maps after 0, 5, 10 and 20 clicks of two simulated clickers that start from the same random first click (star). Top: wells first, then pass. Bottom: the pass suggestion only. White circles: revealed points. The number in each panel is the estimated cis to trans pass (kcal/mol); the value with all points revealed is 43.0.

**Figure 5. Click strategies compared.** Simulated clickers, 40 seeded starts per click rule, on the stored points. (a) Absolute error of the cis to trans pass estimate against the number of clicks (median; band, 25th to 75th percentile, shown for the two focus strategies). The dotted line is the cheap map alone (8.7 kcal/mol) and the green zone is within 1 kcal/mol. (b) Percentage of the starts with the well order cis, trans, nitro as in the all-points map. Wells first reaches 0.9 kcal/mol after 6 clicks but gets the well order right in only 31 of 40 starts; random and spread clicks reach a higher share (33 and 38 of 40) by 20 clicks. The pass suggestion alone does not improve.

**Figure S1. Skala against the cheap methods.** Relative energies of the 306 points below 40 kcal/mol on the Skala map. (a) GFN2-xTB. (b) PBE. Dashed line: the identity shifted by the mean offset; mean absolute difference after removing the offset 4.6 (xTB) and 3.0 kcal/mol (PBE).

**Figure S2. Cost of the Skala run.** (a) Skala time per point on one worker (median 51 s, mean 54 s, maximum 150 s). (b) Cumulative worker-hours in order of completion; 665 points took 10.0 worker-hours (two workers, two threads each).

**Figure S3. Well windows.** The three search windows (rectangles) on the Skala map and clicks 2 to 6 of the 40 wells-first clickers (white points, jittered). The cluster near 110 degrees and 2.0 angstrom is the pass suggestion.
