An interactive web explorer for human-steered sampling of Skala energy maps

A potential energy map costs one expensive calculation per point. This explorer lets a person decide which points to compute next, one click at a time, and shows how the estimate of a barrier changes after every click.

The idea in plain words

  1. Click. You see a cheap map of the energy (GFN2-xTB) and click a place you want to know better.
  2. Reveal. The page shows one Skala energy for that place.
  3. Update. The page corrects the cheap map near the revealed points and re-estimates the barrier, the lowest pass between two named wells.
  4. Suggest. It offers up to four next points from simple rules. You may ignore them and click elsewhere.
  5. Log. Every choice is written to a session log that you can download.

Replay mode. All 665 Skala-1.1 / def2-SVP energies were computed in advance and are stored in this site, so a click reveals one stored value. No quantum-chemistry calculation runs in your browser, and nobody waits for one. The same page design would also work with live calculations.

The example is one proton moving around a nitrite frame (nitrous acid): the map shows where the proton sits, not a reaction path.

The demo and comparison pages use simulated clickers, fixed rules that stand in for a person. No person has been tested yet. The two explorer pages are for you. The click page works best on a phone; the zoom-box page needs a mouse or a trackpad.
